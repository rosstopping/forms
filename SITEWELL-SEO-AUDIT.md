# Sitewell SEO and AI-search audit

**Scope and evidence — 1 October 2026.** I reviewed the current Laravel-rendered public pages, route sitemap, templates, page configuration and visible copy. A local render of all 103 sitemap URLs returned HTTP 200. This is a **code and local-render audit**, not a production crawl: the production host could not be fetched from the available browsing tool. I did not have Search Console, analytics, index coverage, backlink, live ranking, conversion or Core Web Vitals data. Keyword assignments and consolidation candidates below are editorial hypotheses, not measured demand or evidence of traffic loss. The inventory records the final local-rendered page state after the safe changes in this pass.

## Executive summary and current positioning

Sitewell sells **managed website and SEO work for UK businesses**. It audits a site, fixes technical and content problems, improves existing pages, creates useful content and keeps the website working after launch. The homepage stresses customer searches across Google and AI-assisted discovery; the initial conversion is a free search audit, followed by a call with Ross. It is a done-for-you service, not a customer self-service SEO SaaS product. Ross is presented as a Doncaster-based founder and web developer. Website ownership, work carried out for clients and clear updates are the differentiators currently visible. The intended market is UK-wide, with local relevance around Doncaster; that does **not** imply a verified public office or eligibility for a Doncaster local-pack listing.

The five highest-impact findings are:

1. The homepage metadata still described an earlier, broader “managed business websites” offer. The shared title suffix and social titles also reflected the older positioning. These are now updated.
2. The sitemap exposes a broad set of agency, sector, service and article URLs. They have distinct metadata, but the agency software/reseller cluster can confuse the new done-for-you proposition. Keep URLs live while assessing Search Console performance and updating content deliberately.
3. The homepage intentionally sends visitors only to the audit. This protects the chosen conversion path, but it leaves core service pages without homepage contextual links. The journal, service hub and other supporting pages must carry those relationships until the navigation strategy changes.
4. Organization and WebSite identity is now stated in homepage structured data. The About page and article bylines are still thin evidence of expertise: add verified credentials, examples of completed work and customer-approved results when available.
5. Private, expiring audit reports were technically indexable if a link leaked. They now emit `noindex`; the public sitemap remains indexable and is advertised in robots.txt.

## Changes implemented

- Rewrote homepage title and description around managed SEO and website work; kept the existing search-outcome H1 and audit-only CTA.
- Replaced the generic shared title suffix with “Sitewell”; aligned Open Graph titles with HTML titles; added basic Twitter summary metadata and `en-GB` document language. No social image was added because no approved asset exists.
- Added factual `Organization` and `WebSite` homepage schema, using the visible brand, UK service area and published phone number. No unverified `LocalBusiness`, `Person`, `SoftwareApplication`, rating or review claim was added.
- Improved metadata for the About, audit entry and three central service pages. Made the journal byline link to the About page.
- Removed a dormant commented Loom embed from the homepage, while leaving the visible page design and current audit path intact. Updated stale tests that expected that removed embed.
- Corrected two pricing phrases that framed a managed service as software tooling.
- Added `noindex` to private audit report views and a sitemap declaration to robots.txt. Existing public URLs, canonicals and sitemap entries were preserved.

## AI-search and trust findings

The new homepage and schema answer “What is Sitewell?”, “Does it offer SEO?”, “Does it manage websites?” and “Who is it for?” more directly. The home copy mentions Google, ChatGPT and AI answers, but this is **a service aspiration, not proof of placement** in any AI answer. The audit's sample AI checks must remain labelled as samples. Google says AI features still depend on ordinary crawlability, indexability, useful text and supported Search appearance; there is no special AI-only markup requirement. See [Google's AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) and [structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).

The founder is visible with name, role, location and phone, and the About page is now better connected to article bylines. However, it does not yet substantiate years of experience, qualifications, process ownership or client outcomes. Add only evidence Ross can verify: an actual company/legal identity and relationship to Digizu if relevant, named people and roles, dated examples of changes made, permissioned client stories, and measured before/after context with methodology. The `/examples` page should become the strongest proof page once those materials exist. Current named client references on sector pages should be checked for permission and precise wording; do not turn names into invented endorsements.

The public FAQ and commercial pages supply extractable answers. Keep answers specific and visible. `FAQPage` markup appears on 15 pages and matches visible questions in the templates reviewed; Google limits FAQ rich results largely to eligible government and health sites, so it should not be treated as a growth tactic. [Google FAQ changes](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes). Existing agency breadcrumb and article markup are useful where they reflect visible page hierarchy and dates. Avoid adding `Service` or `Person` markup until the visible service/person details are complete enough to match it. Avoid `LocalBusiness` without a verified public business location and identity. No existing `SoftwareApplication` schema was found on the public inventory.

## Technical and content findings

- **Indexability:** All 103 sitemap URLs returned 200 locally; each has a unique title, description, H1 and self-referencing canonical in the rendered output. Public pages have no `noindex`. Private audit reports now do. This does not verify that Google indexed them or that production serves identical output.
- **Discovery:** The XML sitemap enumerates 103 public pages. `robots.txt` now points to it. Main-content link analysis found `/how-it-works`, `/examples` and `/faqs` with no inbound link from another page's `<main>` in the sampled render; FAQs is linked from the footer. This is **not** proof of full orphans, but important pages merit contextual links where naturally relevant. The homepage's audit-only CTA decision is retained.
- **Paid-search variants:** `/local-seo`, `/managed-seo` and `/small-business-seo` are live PPC pages outside the sitemap, each canonicalised to its matching service page; they are not redirects. Their main-content links should point directly to the preferred service URL unless campaign tracking requires the PPC path. Verify production canonical output and whether the variants serve distinct paid-search conversion needs. Query-string plan links, agency fragments, the WordPress download and call-booking URLs are deliberately outside the sitemap.
- **Templates:** Public content is server-rendered HTML; the audit form and results use JavaScript enhancements but basic marketing content is present without client rendering. The home SVG search marks use empty alt text because their adjacent text labels identify the services. The audit screenshot has empty alt text and should receive a short descriptive alt if it conveys the tested site's identity; it is private and does not affect organic discovery.
- **Media and performance:** The homepage currently omits the old branded Loom video; the How it works page still embeds Loom lazily. Test production Core Web Vitals, third-party video impact, mobile layout and font loading with field/Chrome data before prioritising changes. Do not infer pass/fail from source alone.
- **Metadata:** Titles and descriptions are unique, but snippets and title links may be rewritten by Google. Two agency titles exceed 65 characters and eight descriptions exceed 160; these are review cues, not defects or hard Google limits. Prioritise accuracy and intent over arbitrary character caps. [Google title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link), [snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).
- **Content:** The service hub and central service pages explain the service. Many industry pages have repeated layouts and generic SEO phrases; check each against actual expertise and search demand before expanding, merging or noindexing. Agency pages such as “SEO reseller platform” and “white-label SEO software” may describe a separate beta offer, but they clash with the main managed-service message if readers cannot distinguish the audiences. Pricing retains plan-specific enrolment language; verify actual commercial terms before any wider rewrite.
- **Canonical host:** The local app emits its request host (`sitewell.test`) as canonical. Validate production `APP_URL`, proxy HTTPS handling and www/non-www redirects so production canonical, Open Graph URL and sitemap all resolve to `https://sitewell.digizu.co.uk`. [Google canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

1. **Evidence-led examples:** Rework `/examples` around 2–3 permissioned, dated examples: initial problem, changes actually made, timeframe, measurement method and limits. This supports both conversion and AI citability more than another generic SEO page.
2. **Clear service boundaries:** Tighten the management/maintenance/support cluster and the managed SEO/small-business SEO cluster with concrete inclusions, exclusions, deliverables and audience differences. Keep existing URLs until performance data shows a true duplicate.
3. **A real methodology page or section:** Use `/how-it-works` to explain audit → prioritisation → implementation → reporting, with named responsibility and factual examples. Remove or replace the Loom embed when Ross supplies the replacement video.
4. **AI visibility explainer:** Expand the existing AI-assistants journal page with exactly what the sample audit can observe, what cannot be ranked reliably across LLMs, and how conventional SEO/content authority helps. Build a commercial GEO page only after a defined service and customer evidence exist.
5. **Selective sector depth:** Improve sectors with genuine client work, clear decision journeys and original examples first. Do not multiply industry pages for keyword variants.
